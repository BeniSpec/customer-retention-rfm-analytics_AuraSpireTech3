# Day 6 — Findings & Recommendations

## Summary
This project analyzed 541,909 transactions from 4,334 customers (UK online retailer, Dec 2010–Dec 2011) to answer one core business question: who comes back, who doesn't, and what should the business do about it.

## Key findings
**1. Retention drops off sharply after the first purchase.**
The retention curve (Day 4) shows the steepest drop happens in month 1 — most cohorts lose the majority of their customers within the first month after their initial purchase, then the decline levels off for those who stick around.

**2. A third of customers never return.**
1,505 customers (34.7%) are one-and-done — they made exactly one purchase and never came back. The remaining 2,829 (65.3%) are repeat customers.

**3. Repeat customers drive almost all the revenue.**
Repeat customers generated £8,132,096.98 (92.8% of total revenue), while one-and-done customers generated only £628,969.67 (7.2%). Retaining customers matters far more than acquiring new ones for this business.

**4. Most repeat customers come back within ~2 months, but a long tail takes up to a year.**
Median time between purchases: 51 days. Average: 76.7 days (pulled up by slower repeat buyers). Breakdown: 424 customers repurchase within a week, 534 within a month, 962 within 3 months, and 909 take longer than 3 months (up to 365 days).

**5. Revenue and customer value are heavily concentrated in a few segments.**
The RFM segmentation (Day 4) shows Champions and Loyal customers hold a disproportionate share of total revenue relative to their numbers, while At Risk and Hibernating segments represent real but currently inactive value.
